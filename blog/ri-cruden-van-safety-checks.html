RI Cruden: daily van safety checks over WhatsApp from day one.

RI Cruden was the first construction firm to run daily van safety checks over Quickler. Every driver, every shift, in the WhatsApp chat already open on their phone. This is what changed in the first three weeks of the rollout.

The starting point

RI Cruden runs a small fleet of vans for its construction operations across Tayside. Daily walkround checks were captured on the standard paper card — driver fills it in, hands it to the yard manager, card gets filed in a binder. Two problems followed. First, cards were sometimes handed in days late. Second, when a defect was reported, the yard manager often did not see it until after the van had already left the yard.

The firm had looked at dedicated fleet apps. The objection was always the same: drivers were not going to install another app, and the firm did not want to manage another set of logins.

What changed

The rollout started Tuesday 2026-04-21. By the end of the first week, every driver was completing the DVSA-aligned walkround in WhatsApp before pulling out of the yard. Defect photos went directly to the fleet manager. The dashboard showed red/amber/green status per vehicle in real time.

Three defects were caught in the first week before the van left the yard. A brake light out on a Transit (would have been a DVSA point if stopped). A damaged rear strap on a tipper. A low front tyre on the bowser trailer. None of these would have been spotted by the previous paper-card process until the driver was already on site or, worse, in an accident.

What the drivers said

"It's just WhatsApp" was the most common response. The drivers were already messaging the yard team in WhatsApp every day. The walkround landing in the same chat thread did not register as a new tool — it registered as the yard sending them a question. No training was needed. No app downloaded. No login remembered.

The harder conversation, when it happened, was about how seriously to take the question. The fleet manager handled this in person on day one by riding along on a couple of walkrounds and showing how the photos came through to the dashboard. After that the drivers took the photos.

What the fleet manager saw

One dashboard. One row per vehicle. Green meant the driver had completed the walkround and reported no defects. Amber meant the walkround was outstanding. Red meant a defect had been reported and the vehicle was held.

The previous process gave the manager a binder full of paper cards at the end of each week. The new process gives them live status on every vehicle in the yard.

What the DVSA audit trail looks like

Every walkround produces a timestamped PDF carrying the driver's answers and any photos. The PDFs are stored against the vehicle and the date. A CSV export of the full register is downloadable from the dashboard at any time. In an audit, this is what gets handed over.

What is next

RI Cruden's next workflow on the platform is RAMS delivery for site operatives. The same logic applies — operatives already use WhatsApp; a dedicated safety app would not have been adopted.

For other construction firms considering the same

The rollout took under a week from sign-up to first live walkround. The single biggest predictor of success in the first week was the fleet manager taking thirty minutes to show two or three drivers how the photos arrived in the dashboard. After that, adoption was automatic.
