iAuditor alternatives for UK firms in 2026.

iAuditor is the market leader. That does not make it the right tool for every UK firm. This page covers why UK field services firms look for alternatives, what the main options are, and what the honest tradeoffs are between them.

Key takeaways
  • iAuditor's biggest problem is not cost — it is engineer adoption. Engineers complete forms from memory in the van, not on site
  • Per-seat pricing means the office manager who reads reports once a week costs as much as the engineer who files four a day
  • The main UK alternatives are GoAudits, Lumiform, and WhatsApp-based tools like Quickler
  • If your engineers already use WhatsApp (they do), a WhatsApp-native tool requires zero training
  • The free plan is limited to 5 templates and does not suit firms running multiple workflows

Why UK firms look for iAuditor alternatives

iAuditor is made by SafetyCulture, an Australian company founded in 2004. They have 85,000 organisations using their platform across 95 countries. The product works. The templates are thorough. The reports look professional.

The complaints that drive UK firms to look for alternatives are consistent. They show up across Capterra, G2, and in conversations with H&S managers who have tried to roll it out.

Engineer adoption. The app requires installation, login, and template selection before the engineer can start. On a job site at 7am with a van to unload, this is not what happens. What happens is the engineer does the inspection from memory and fills in iAuditor in the car park at the end. The record says it was completed. It was not completed on site.

Per-seat pricing. Every person who needs access is a seat. The engineer is a seat. The office manager is a seat. The director who looks at reports once a quarter is a seat. A firm with 10 engineers and 5 office staff pays for 15 seats. The pricing is not unreasonable for large enterprises. For UK SMEs with mixed teams, it adds up quickly.

Template setup. iAuditor's template library contains thousands of templates. None of them are your template. Building your firm's specific checklist takes time, and the platform is complex enough that someone needs to own it. For firms without a dedicated operations manager, this is a burden that often results in the default template being used, which does not match the work being done.

App crashes. Reported consistently across review platforms. On site, with a client present, an app crash is not a minor inconvenience. It is a reason to go back to paper.

The main alternatives

GoAudits

GoAudits is the closest direct alternative. Similar feature set, lower price point (from around £10 per seat per month), and a larger UK customer base than most people realise — they have been bootstrapping since 2016 and have real traction in UK hospitality, retail and construction.

The same structural problem applies: it is an app. Engineers install it. Engineers forget the login. Engineers complete forms from memory. The template library is excellent. The reporting is clean. But the engineer adoption problem does not go away because the app is cheaper.

Lumiform

A German-built inspection app with UK customers. Similar to iAuditor in structure. Often cited as cheaper for smaller teams. Less mature product, smaller template library, less established support infrastructure in the UK.

Paper and spreadsheets

Still the most common approach for UK SMEs. Free to start. Familiar. No training required. Produces records that are illegible, unsearchable, and impossible to surface when DVSA or HSE ask for evidence. The cost is not the subscription — it is the time chasing engineers for forms and the liability when records are missing.

Quickler

Quickler is the only option in this list that does not require engineers to install anything or learn anything. The inspection workflow arrives in their existing WhatsApp chat. They reply to each question as a message. Photos attach in the same way they would to any WhatsApp conversation. The completed report generates as a PDF.

The pricing is per firm, not per seat: £50 per month for up to 1 engineer and 3 workflows, £100 for up to 4 engineers, £140 for up to 12, £300 for up to 30. The office manager, the director, and anyone else with dashboard access does not add to the cost.

The honest limitation: Quickler does not have iAuditor's template library or its depth of features. It is built for firms with 1–30 field engineers doing structured site compliance work — van checks, site inspections, H&S assessments — not for enterprise quality management programmes across hundreds of sites.

How to choose

The right question is not which tool has more features. It is which tool your engineers will actually complete on site.

If your engineers are desk-based, comfortable with new software, and completing inspections in environments with reliable internet — iAuditor or GoAudits are mature, well-supported products that will serve you well.

If your engineers are in vans, working in conditions where pulling out a phone to open an app feels like extra admin, and you have already tried iAuditor and found adoption dropped off — a WhatsApp-native workflow is worth testing. The engineers will use it because they already use WhatsApp for everything else.

One thing to check before switching: does the alternative produce a report format your clients and regulators will accept? PDF output from any reputable tool should be fine for most purposes, but if you are in a sector with specific format requirements, verify before committing.

Frequently asked questions

Why do engineers stop using iAuditor?

The most common reason: the app adds friction at the point when engineers have tools in their hands. Login, template selection, photo upload — these take time that feels unreasonable on site. Engineers start completing forms in the van afterwards, from memory. The record exists but does not reflect what was actually observed in real time.

How much does iAuditor cost for a UK firm with 10 engineers?

SafetyCulture's pricing changes — check their current pricing page for the exact figure. The cost scales per seat and typically reaches £150–£250 per month for a team of 10 engineers plus office staff on a standard plan. Quickler charges £140 per month for up to 12 engineers, regardless of how many office staff have dashboard access.

What is the main difference between iAuditor and Quickler?

iAuditor is a dedicated inspection app. Quickler runs in WhatsApp. Engineers using iAuditor install the app, log in, and complete forms inside it. Engineers using Quickler receive questions in their existing WhatsApp chat and reply to them. iAuditor has a larger template library and more configuration options. Quickler has higher on-site completion rates because engineers already know how to use WhatsApp.

Is there a free iAuditor alternative in the UK?

iAuditor has a free plan capped at 5 templates. GoAudits has a limited free tier. Quickler offers a free trial. For firms running compliance reporting across multiple workflows at scale, free plans are not sufficient — the meaningful comparison is between paid tiers.
