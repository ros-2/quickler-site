Template + how-to

Structural engineer site inspection report template.

A repeatable template UK structural engineers can run over WhatsApp during the site visit. Built so the structured draft report is ready by the time the engineer is back at the desk. Designed around how IStructE members already write up site inspections.

The problem the template solves

A site visit produces dozens of photos, half a notebook of sketches, a recorded voice memo, and a head full of professional judgement. The engineer drives back, opens the office laptop, and rebuilds the inspection from this raw material into a formatted IStructE-style report. Most engineers count this as the slowest part of their working week.

The Quickler structural template captures the inspection in the same order the engineer walks the building. Photos, voice notes and measurements go into the right sections of the draft report as the engineer captures them. By the time they are back at the desk, the structured draft is ready to review and sign.

Template structure

  1. Project header. Client, address, date of visit, weather, instructing party, scope of inspection.
  2. Building description. Form of construction, age (estimated or known), occupancy, any prior reports referenced. Engineer captures by voice note; Quickler transcribes.
  3. Elevation walk. One subsection per elevation. Each carries: overall photo, defect photos with annotation, measurement readings (lean, deflection, crack width), engineer commentary.
  4. Internal walk. Room by room or zone by zone, captured in the same pattern.
  5. Specific defects. Each defect gets: location, photograph, measurement (if applicable), severity classification (cosmetic / monitoring / progressive / urgent), recommended action.
  6. Conclusions. Engineer's professional opinion captured as voice note, transcribed automatically.
  7. Recommended next actions. Captured as a structured list rather than free prose so the client can act on them.
  8. Sign-off. The accountable engineer reviews the draft in the dashboard, edits in place, and signs.

What the engineer sees on site

The engineer sees WhatsApp. Each section is prompted by a message. Photos taken in the phone camera are dropped into the chat in reply. Sketches are photographed. Voice notes are spoken straight into the chat — the transcription appears in the draft report before the engineer reaches the next elevation.

There is no app to open, no form to fill, no separate camera roll to pick from later. The phone the engineer already has becomes the inspection tool.

Sign-off and professional accountability

The Quickler workflow generates the draft. The accountable engineer reviews, edits and signs. The signature is the professional engineer's, not Quickler's. The audit trail records every captured message, every photo, every voice note, every edit before sign-off.

Frequently asked questions

Does the report meet IStructE expectations?

The template structure follows the order IStructE members already use in site-visit reports — header, building description, elevations, internal, defects, conclusions, recommendations. The signed report is the engineer's professional output, exactly as today.

Can the firm customise the template?

Yes. Each firm's first onboarding step is to translate their existing site-visit report format into a Quickler workflow. Templates can vary per inspection type — full structural survey, defect-only inspection, party wall, monitoring visit.

How are measurements captured?

Numeric measurements (crack width, lean, deflection, dimension) are captured as typed responses. Photographic evidence of the measurement is captured alongside. Drawings and sketches are photographed and attached to the relevant defect.

What about CAD files or IFC models?

v0.1 of the format does not yet handle CAD/IFC attachments inline — they are stored as job attachments. CAD/IFC handling is on the v0.2 roadmap for the open format spec at github.com/ros-2/compliance-workflow-spec.

Does this work for party wall inspections?

Yes. The party wall inspection is one of the workflow variants. The template captures the location, the works being assessed, the existing condition of each elevation, and the recommended schedule of condition.
