RAMS and toolbox talks delivered over WhatsApp.

UK construction firms need every operative to acknowledge every RAMS briefing and every toolbox talk. Paper signature sheets get lost; safety apps get ignored. WhatsApp gets read. This page covers how Quickler delivers RAMS and toolbox talks to operatives in the chat already on their phone.

The acknowledgement problem

RAMS (Risk Assessments and Method Statements) and toolbox talks are mandatory inputs to UK construction H&S. The output everyone tracks is the same: every named operative on the site that day has read, understood, and acknowledged the brief. The signature sheet has to be produced if the HSE asks.

Paper signature sheets get scrunched up in jackets and lost. Dedicated safety apps get ignored because operatives have to install, log in, and remember to open them. WhatsApp gets opened forty times a day on every operative's phone.

The Quickler workflow

  1. Site supervisor uploads the RAMS or toolbox talk document and the list of operatives expected on site that day.
  2. Each operative receives the document on WhatsApp at the start of their shift along with the brief in chat-readable form.
  3. The chat asks one or two understanding-check questions in plain English. The operative replies.
  4. The operative is asked to confirm acknowledgement — yes or no. The reply is timestamped and stored against the operative and the site.
  5. The site supervisor's dashboard shows green for acknowledged, amber for outstanding, red for refused. Refusals trigger an immediate supervisor alert.
  6. The site acknowledgement register is downloadable as a PDF or CSV at any time for HSE evidence.

What the operative sees

The operative sees WhatsApp. The brief lands as a normal message. The understanding-check question comes as another message. They reply yes or no. The whole process takes under a minute.

For operatives whose first language is not English, the brief can be delivered translated. v0.2 of the format spec covers multilingual delivery — currently English is the production option, with Welsh, Gaelic and Polish on the roadmap.

HSE-ready evidence

The PDF register carries the site, the date, the brief reference, every operative's name, their acknowledgement timestamp, and their understanding-check answers. The HSE inspector gets a single document that proves who knew what, when.

Frequently asked questions

How is this acknowledgement legally binding?

The operative's WhatsApp reply is timestamped, attributed to their phone number, and stored unaltered. The audit trail is no weaker than a paper signature. The CDM 2015 regulations require the operative to have understood the brief — Quickler captures both the brief and the operative's acknowledgement.

Can we add a photo or video to the brief?

Yes. RAMS attachments — photos of the site hazards, short videos of the safe method — are sent in the chat alongside the brief.

What if an operative refuses to acknowledge?

The refusal is recorded and the supervisor receives an immediate alert. The site supervisor can then either re-deliver the brief in person, change the operative's task, or escalate.

How does this handle agency operatives or last-minute additions?

New operatives are added to the site list from the dashboard at any time. The brief is sent to their WhatsApp the moment they are added; the dashboard waits for their acknowledgement before showing the site as fully briefed.

Can we deliver the brief in languages other than English?

English is the production option in v0.1. Welsh, Gaelic and Polish are on the v0.2 roadmap. Multilingual delivery is tracked in the open format spec at github.com/ros-2/compliance-workflow-spec.
