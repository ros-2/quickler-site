Risk assessment software for UK small firms: what to use in 2026.

An honest guide for the H&S manager or director at a 5–50 person UK firm. Covers what a risk assessment must include by law, when Word templates are enough, and what dedicated software actually adds.

Key takeaways
  • Firms with five or more employees must record their risk assessment findings in writing under the Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999
  • A clear Word document meets the legal requirement — format is not prescribed
  • Dedicated RA software adds value when assessment volume is high or field workers need to record dynamic assessments in real time
  • Per-seat pricing from general inspection tools (iAuditor, GoAudits) can be expensive for firms with mixed office-and-field teams
  • Dynamic risk assessments for field workers are the area where WhatsApp-based tools offer the most practical advantage over paper or apps

What the law requires

The Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999 require every employer to make a "suitable and sufficient" assessment of risks to employees and others who may be affected by the work. The duty exists regardless of firm size.

For firms with five or more employees, the findings must be recorded in writing. The regulations do not prescribe a format. A Word document, a spreadsheet, a paper form, or dedicated software — all are legally equivalent if they cover the required content.

The required content is: the hazards identified, who might be harmed and how, the existing control measures, the residual risk level, and the actions needed. The assessment must be reviewed when conditions change — after an accident, after a significant change in work activity, or at regular intervals.

There is no requirement to use a specific risk matrix, a specific scoring system, or any particular software vendor. The HSE provides free templates on its website. For firms doing standard, repeatable work in predictable environments, those templates are adequate.

When Word and Excel are enough

For a firm doing similar work across similar sites — a small electrical contractor, a plumbing business, a cleaning company — a set of well-maintained Word templates reviewed annually will meet the legal requirement. The HSE's own templates cover the five-point process. They cost nothing. They are readable by anyone. They print cleanly.

Word and Excel fall short in three specific situations. First, when the volume of assessments means version control becomes a problem — you have twelve versions of the same template across twelve engineers' laptops and no way to know which is current. Second, when assessments need to be completed on site rather than in the office. Third, when field workers need to record deviations from the planned assessment as conditions change — the dynamic risk assessment use case.

If none of those three situations apply to your firm, the honest advice is to stick with templates and spend the money on something else.

Dedicated risk assessment software

Dedicated RA tools — including platforms like Evalu-8, Sypol, and others aimed at UK SMEs — solve the version control and access problems. Assessments live in one place. Updates propagate to all users. The office can see which assessments have been reviewed and which are overdue.

The weakness is adoption. Getting field workers to use a new system is hard. Most dedicated RA tools are built for the H&S manager filling in assessments at a desk, not for the engineer on a rooftop deciding how to handle an unplanned hazard. The mobile apps are often afterthoughts.

Pricing varies considerably. Some tools charge per assessment. Some charge per user. Some require annual contracts. For a 20-person firm where only three people write assessments, per-user pricing on a large platform is poor value.

General inspection apps: iAuditor and GoAudits

iAuditor (SafetyCulture) and GoAudits are not risk assessment tools in the strict sense. They are inspection and audit platforms that can be configured for risk assessments among many other uses. If your firm already uses iAuditor for site inspections, adding risk assessment templates to the same tool is reasonable.

The structural problem with both tools is per-seat pricing. iAuditor charges per user. An office manager who approves one risk assessment a month pays the same as an engineer who files four a week. For firms with a typical mix of office and field staff, this adds up.

Both tools require an app install. Both require training. Reviews from UK tradespeople on Capterra consistently flag the same issues: template setup takes longer than expected, the apps are unreliable on older Android devices, and engineers revert to paper when the app plays up on site.

WhatsApp-based tools for dynamic risk assessments

Dynamic risk assessments are the area where WhatsApp-based tools offer the most practical advantage. A dynamic RA is a real-time assessment carried out by a field worker when site conditions deviate from what was planned. The hazard profile is different. The planned controls may not apply. The worker needs to record what they found and what they decided to do before starting work.

Asking a worker on a rooftop, in a confined space, or in a flooded basement to open an app, log in, find the right template, and fill in a form is not realistic. Sending them a short sequence of questions over WhatsApp — a channel they already use — is.

The dynamic RA use case is not a replacement for the firm's written risk assessment programme. It sits alongside it, capturing the gap between the planned assessment and what the engineer actually found on site. That gap is where most accidents happen.

Frequently asked questions

What must a risk assessment include under UK law?

Under the Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999, a risk assessment must identify hazards, decide who might be harmed and how, evaluate risks and decide on precautions, record findings if you have five or more employees, and be reviewed and updated when circumstances change. There is no prescribed format. A clear Word document covering these points is legally equivalent to dedicated software. The HSE provides free templates on its website.

Do small firms with fewer than five employees need a written risk assessment?

No. The requirement to record findings in writing applies only to firms with five or more employees. Smaller firms must still carry out risk assessments — the duty is universal — they just do not have to write them down. In practice, a written record is always worth keeping: it demonstrates due diligence, helps onboard new workers, and is essential if a legal challenge arises.

What is a dynamic risk assessment and who needs one?

A dynamic risk assessment is a real-time assessment carried out by a field worker when site conditions change and the pre-planned risk assessment no longer fits. It is common across all field trades — electricians, plumbers, roofers, engineers — where every site is different and hazards emerge during the job. A dynamic RA supplements the written risk assessment; it does not replace it. Workers should be trained on when to apply one and how to record what they found and what controls they applied.

Is Word or Excel adequate for risk assessments at a small UK firm?

Yes, for most firms with predictable, repeatable work. The HSE provides free Word templates that meet the legal requirement. Software adds value when assessment volume is high, work varies significantly between sites, or field workers need to record dynamic assessments in real time. For a firm with a standard set of activities and a small team, well-maintained Word templates reviewed annually are entirely adequate.
